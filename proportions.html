Proportions

Choosing dimensions without deciding each one.

01How to read this volume

Proportion is how a designer avoids choosing every dimension from scratch. Each system here is a species: a way of generating sizes that belong together. They are not rival religions. Most objects use two at once: a module for what must fit, and a ratio for what must look right.

Numbers marked [verify] are starting points, not laws. The golden ratio has been marked [verify] by history itself.

02Three questions
02.1Does it have to fit something? A hand, a rack, a drawer, another part. Then the size comes from outside, and a module or the human scale decides it. 02.2Does it come in sizes? A family of parts (small, medium, large) needs steps that feel even. That is the job of preferred numbers and of √2. 02.3Is it only about how it looks? Then use a ratio: simple ratios for calm, √2 for documents, the golden ratio if you must.

Why R10 steps by 1.26. R10 means ten steps per decade. Every step multiplies by the same factor q, and ten steps must take 1 to 10: q¹⁰ = 10, so q = 10^(1/10) ≈ 1.259. The same rule gives R5 (10^(1/5) ≈ 1.585) and R20 (10^(1/20) ≈ 1.122).

The exact values are rounded to friendly numbers, never more than about 1 % off; 3.162 becomes 3.15, close to π. Steps are equal in percentage, not in millimetres, because people and tolerances judge size by ratio. Three R10 steps double a size (10^(3/10) ≈ 1.995), the product of two R10 values is another R10 value, and one R10 step is exactly 1 dB of power ratio. The eye and the ear agree on this one. ↩
